03A day in data

Twenty-four hours, read slowly.

A wearable produces thousands of numbers a day. Scroll through one sample day and watch how the signals respond to ordinary things: light, meals, a walk, a long afternoon, sleep.

  1. 06:30

    Wake, then find daylight

    Morning light is one of the strongest cues for the body clock. Heart rate climbs gently as the body shifts out of sleep.

  2. 08:00

    Breakfast

    A rise in glucose after eating is normal. Its shape depends on the meal, the person and even the night before.

  3. 12:45

    A walk after lunch

    Many studies find that a short walk after a meal is associated with a smaller glucose rise. Steps spike and heart rate follows.

  4. 15:00

    The afternoon dip

    For many people alertness dips in the early afternoon. It is part of the daily rhythm, not a personal failing.

  5. 19:30

    Dinner and winding down

    A later, larger meal shows up in the glucose trace. Dimmer light in the evening lets the body clock move towards night.

  6. 23:15

    Sleep

    Heart rate settles through the night. Deep sleep tends to cluster earlier in the night and REM sleep later.

04Signal vs noise

One reading is a rumour. A trend is a story.

Daily numbers bounce around for dull reasons: a late meal, a warm room, a cold coming on. Switch the views to see how averages and typical ranges turn noise into something you can actually read.

Resting heart rate

Usually measured during sleep or first thing in the morning. A single high reading after a late dinner means little; a rise that lasts for several days is worth noticing.
